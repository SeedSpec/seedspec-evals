# Evidence published with SeedSpec 0.2

SeedSpec 0.2 publishes a capable evaluation machine and a deliberately small
set of findings. This is not a claim that the benchmark suite is comprehensive
or that passing it certifies a seed, skill, model, or realization.

## Specialized authoring guidance

In one repeated synthetic warehouse-transfer authorship comparison,
specialized domain guidance covered 33 of 36 shared obligation observations
versus 14 of 36 without that knowledge. It aligned 21 of 21 material-decision
observations versus 10 of 21. Embedding the same knowledge directly in trusted
instructions was nearly as strong.

Responsible conclusion: making relevant expertise available and reliably
consulted can materially help. The evidence does not show that the file type
caused the improvement or that every packaged skill helps.

## Implementation skills and model capability

In the completed 2×2 implementation matrix, stronger procedural guidance
improved the lower-cost model:

- covered shared obligations increased from 9 to 16 of 24;
- aligned shared decisions increased from 8 to 12 of 18; and
- deviations decreased from 7 to 5.

The guidance did not make that model equivalent to the stronger model. All six
lower-cost-model runs retained a critical forgeable-identity deviation, even
when their skill-guided self-review declared the boundary clear.

Responsible conclusion: a strong procedure can amplify model capability and
improve evidence coverage, but it does not reliably replace judgment.
Independent evaluation remains necessary.

## Benchmark saturation

All twelve implementations in that matrix received 9 of 10 from the legacy
deterministic contract score, despite the independent evaluator finding the
critical identity deviation in all six lower-cost-model runs.

The deterministic result is therefore labeled a contract/integrity gate, not
an implementation-quality score. Security, reliability, maintainability,
flexibility, test depth, and evidence quality remain separate observations.
